# Scenario backlog: what to film first and why

Directions for the next batch of Elementors shorts, not scenarios. Each row becomes a `/scenario` run when it is picked. Built 2026-10-13 from Indie Game Luke's "How 100 Indie Games Went Viral on TikTok" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pg_cZsMgBD0, transcript in `docs/research/indie_game_luke_transcript.txt`), its data sheet (https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Zir-luvLeZ2qw1T6gDzxh50x8EFFVRphNqgFT-2KUGM), and the extra sources at the end. Existing rules in `.claude/skills/scenario/references/hooks.md` still apply on top of everything here.

## What the video and the sheet say

55 videos, 54 games, all Steam or PC indies, "viral" meaning over 100k views. Wishlists are the author's estimate from Steam follower curves on SteamDB, not reported data. The genres that kept showing up: cozy/farming, social co-op horror, building, horror, survival, car and truck sims. The genres the author did not see at all: strategy (tower defense, RTS, turn-based), puzzle, narrative. An autobattler sits in the missing bucket, and his own answer to that is that uniqueness beats genre: a distinct look, a distinct mechanic, an interesting spell or action. Everything below is a hypothesis for us, not a benchmark.

The author's takeaways, from the transcript and the "Video Notes" tab: gameplay is king; funny gets views and does not convert (Watch Out For Goblins, 1.3M views, about 18 Steam followers); a game that goes viral for the game itself goes viral 10 to 50 times, a game that goes viral for a joke usually does not repeat; length did not correlate with anything, a 58 s talk-over and a 2 min pure gameplay clip (M.I.N.S.K.) both worked, "show 15 seconds, show 30, show two to three minutes"; progress and before/after is his single strongest recommendation, and it worked for a plain-looking game too (Mr. Moo: a 2M-view dev meme gave about 80 wishlists, a 500k-view "five years of development" cut about 1,000); early vs late game works; a ridiculous mechanic works, and a small one added for the clip is fine (a spinning road sign to trick-shot off); a feedback question works; comparing to a popular game works when it is two games plus a twist, a straight lookalike goes viral for the wrong reason; nostalgia for a mechanic from a childhood game works (Sea of Stars timed hits, 1.2M); milestones (demo out, festival feature) are worth a cut every time; responding to negative comments makes people root for you, and the comments do not have to be real for it to work; no voice needed, text or TTS is fine; no correlation with music, trendy sounds, CapCut or posting frequency, 5-10 posts a month is enough; test each style two or three times before dropping it, the same cut got 10k views once and 1M the next time; a personal account for a solo or duo team reads as "real indie" and gets rooted for; cross-post, Plunder did 4-5M on YouTube Shorts with videos that did a few hundred thousand on TikTok; dev tips, devlogs, festival and "indie dev life" memes get views from other devs and convert a quarter to a tenth as well.

Conversion math from the video: about 290 Steam followers per 1M views, 5-10 wishlists per follower, so roughly 2,000-2,500 wishlists per 1M views, 0.2-0.25% of views. Zukowski's threshold for waking the Steam algorithm is about 3,500 wishlists in a short window, so a cut needs 500k-1M views to matter on its own. Paid boosting looked weak: Dive the Depths bought its way to 2.3M views with 10k likes and about 1,000 wishlists, Loddlenaut had 2.3M organic views with 600k likes and twice the wishlists. Game-discovery creators (MadMorph, Brax finds games) gave 1M-view clips to games whose own posts got a few hundred.

Outside sources agree on the frame: hook inside 1 s, the concept clear by 3-5 s, 10-30 s total, loop seamless, a screen recording with TTS beat polished production, re-edit and repost the same moment (The Matriarch: 10k views vs 7M for a near-identical video after a re-cut at 130% speed), video replies to comments get pinned and lift both videos, custom cuts beat reposted trailers. Presskit's "what fails" list names spreadsheet-style strategy and depth-over-visuals games, which is the risk for an autobattler: the short has to sell the visible event, never the decision.

## Where to put the weight

- Pure gameplay from frame 0, every cut. The event the camera punches on is the product: impact, cascade, reveal, freeze, melt. The strategy never gets explained.
- Wave 1 first and all of it: eight cuts from five renders plus edits. The set says games that win on gameplay win repeatedly, so the first batch is about finding which of our events repeats.
- Every render ships as two cuts: streamer voice and silent text-only. The set has silent hits with higher conversion than voiced ones (M.I.N.S.K., Tiny Glade, Sea of Stars), and the text cut costs an edit, not a render.
- One sentence of what the game is, said the way a player says it, in the pitch and the design-rule cuts (k, l, e). Those are the templates with the best conversion in the sheet.
- 10-25 s stays the default for the streamer cuts, but length is not a rule in the data: one long uncut round (z) goes into the first month as a test.
- Each style gets two or three tries before it is dropped. One post is not a test.
- The link lives in the bio, not in the video. CTA in bio made no difference to conversion; no link at all killed it.
- Repost the winners with a small change (speed, first line, caption) instead of inventing a new moment every time.
- Every cut goes to YouTube Shorts and Reels the next day, no watermark.
- Track followers per 1M views (about 290 in the set) next to views; views alone said nothing about wishlists.
- Beauty, cozy and funny are not our lanes. If a funny cut happens, it is a bonus, not a direction.
- The progress series (u) is the one template the author rates above everything, and it needs a different voice from the stream clips: first person, "we", the struggle on screen. Decide whether it runs on the game account or a personal one before writing it.

## Open

- No autobattler or strategy game in the set. The nearest evidence for us is the mechanic hooks (Bionic Bay, RECUR, Sea of Stars), so k, e, l, y are the cuts that test whether our mechanic reads in 3 s.
- Wave 2 rows q, r, t assume a TikTok account that gets comments; none of them should be written before the first batch is posted.
